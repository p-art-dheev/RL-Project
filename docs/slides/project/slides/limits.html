Limitations

What we did not solve, and what we would do next

Large analytics DAGs

The held-out jobs are bigger (median 25 vs 15 tasks). The pure policy overloads the green region with them; the shield closes most of the gap to the best heuristic. Next: a GNN job encoder, and training on larger DAGs.

Carbon data

Hourly profiles are synthetic, built from real annual means (India ≈ 560–680, Stockholm ≈ 35 g/kWh). Free hourly data for India was not available; the code already reads Electricity Maps CSVs.

Energy is modelled

AWS exposes no per-VM power, so energy = measured CPU time × published per-vCPU power coefficients × PUE (the same model class as MERSEM).

Small real testbed

Six nodes, three seeds per load, one campus network. On AWS, PACE at w = 0.9 used 15% more carbon than MERSEM (not significant): there it bought deadlines with carbon.

Say these before the examiner does. The most important one is the first: our learned policy wins on alerts and carbon but, without the shield, misses more large analytics jobs than the tuned heuristic. We diagnosed why (job size distribution shift; the cost one placement imposes on other jobs is not in its own reward) instead of hiding it.
